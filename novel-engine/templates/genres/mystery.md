# Genre module: mystery

Covers whodunits, detective fiction, and the mystery thread inside a hybrid (fantasy mystery, historical mystery).

## Reader promises
- **Fair play.** Every clue the detective uses to solve the case is shown to the reader before the solution.
- **A solution that is surprising and inevitable.** On a reread, the answer was in plain sight.
- **A detective who earns it** by observation, legwork and reasoning, not luck or confession.
- **Red herrings that play fair:** misleading, but honestly explained by the end.
- **The reveal discipline holds:** the culprit shows no tells before the book wants the reader to suspect them.

## Core system
**The crime.** Page: `story/system/the-crime.md` (from templates/pages/system.md, replacing the spec table with this one). When mystery is a secondary genre, this page is still required.
- What really happened, minute by minute, for each crime (the "true timeline"), including what the culprit did before and after.
- Method, motive, opportunity, for the culprit and for each suspect.
- What physical evidence each crime leaves, and who could find it.
- What each witness saw, believes, and says (and why those differ).
- The culprit's cover story and its weak point.
- How the detective could solve it: the chain of deductions, each link tied to a clue.

## Extra lock questions
- **Clue plan:** every clue, the chapter it is planted in, how it is disguised, what it points to, and when the reader should be able to suspect. Fill the clue table in the outline.
- **Red herrings:** each one, why it is plausible, when and how it is cleared.
- **Reveal discipline:** the chapter where the reader may first suspect the culprit; what the culprit is allowed to show before then (nothing that reads as a tell).
- **The culprit's off-page timeline** for every day of the book, regardless of the detective.
- **Victim plan:** names, dates and cadence of each crime; running count of victims.
- **Escalation:** why the culprit acts faster, or more boldly, as the book goes on.
- **The proof:** what convinces the authorities (or the reader) at the end. A confession alone is not enough.

## Story folders
- `story/system/the-crime.md`
- `story/characters/` with a suspects section in the outline
- `story/evidence.md` (optional): every piece of evidence, where it is, who has seen it. If the book has no separate crime page or evidence page, checks use the outline's clue and payoff plan and continuity.md instead.

## Research targets
- The model authors' talks and essays on plotting mysteries and fair play.
- Procedure for the setting: how investigations, arrests, inquests and evidence worked there and then (or how the invented world's equivalent works).

## Chapter checks
- **Fair play:** any deduction that uses a clue not yet shown to the reader.
- **Tells:** any line that makes the culprit suspicious before the planned chapter (lingering, unprompted alibis, odd reactions).
- **Knowledge slips:** a detective or witness knowing something they couldn't yet.
- **Evidence continuity:** who holds each item, where it is, what it says, matches the evidence page and earlier chapters.
- **Victim and timeline counts** match continuity.md.
