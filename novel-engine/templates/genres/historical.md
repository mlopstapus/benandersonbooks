# Genre module: historical fiction

Fiction set in a real time and place. The story and many characters are invented; the period is not. (Fantasy that only *echoes* a period uses the fantasy module, with a period note.)

## Reader promises
- **Immersion:** the period feels lived-in through daily detail: work, food, clothes, money, travel, how people speak and what they assume.
- **Accuracy where it counts,** and honesty where the book departs from the record (an author's historical note).
- **Real history matters to the plot,** not just the backdrop.
- **Characters with period minds:** their beliefs and blind spots belong to their time, without the narrator endorsing them.

## Core system
**The period.** Pages: `story/research/` (one page per topic, from templates/pages/research-topic.md) and `story/system/the-period.md` (the hard constraints). Spec questions for the period page:
- Exact years, place, and the real events the book overlaps.
- Real people who appear or are mentioned: policy for each (on the page, mentioned only, invented stand-in).
- Hard constraints that shape the plot: how long travel and messages take, what medicine can and can't do, the law and police procedure, money and prices, who can go where (class, gender, race, religion).
- Technology and objects available, and the anachronism traps (things that feel period but aren't yet).
- How people speak: register, terms of address, oaths; the policy for how much period language reaches the page (`period_words` in book.yaml).
- **Sources and citations:** which sources are trusted, how claims are cited (every claim on a research page cites a note in `raw/history/`), and whether the book will include a historical note or bibliography.
- **Fact vs. invention ledger:** for every real-world element the book uses, mark real, invented or altered. Kept on each research page and summarized on the period page.

## Extra lock questions
- The real calendar: actual weekdays for the book's dates, holidays, moon and weather where known, any real events on those days.
- Real places: which exist (and what they were like then), which are invented, and how invented ones fit the real map.
- Real people: what they did on the book's dates; the book must not contradict the record without marking it altered.
- Prices and wages for the things characters buy and earn; running counts of money if it matters.
- Attitudes: the period beliefs that will appear on the page, and how the narration frames them.

## Story folders
- `story/research/` (cited topic pages)
- `story/system/the-period.md`
- `raw/history/` (one note per source: full citation, what was taken from it, link)
- `story/world/` (places, idioms-and-terms with period terms, sets)

## Research targets
- Primary sources where practical (period newspapers, diaries, maps, directories), noted with citations.
- Reputable secondary histories of the place and period.
- The model authors' talks or essays on researching historical fiction.
- Research runs per topic and is saved as it goes. When a fact can't be confirmed, say so and mark it on the ledger.

## Chapter checks
- **Anachronisms:** words, idioms, objects, technology, attitudes and names that don't belong to the period. Quote and suggest a period alternative.
- **Fact vs. invention:** every real-world claim in the chapter is on a research page, or is flagged as new and needing a source.
- **Real calendar:** weekdays, holidays and real events on the chapter's dates.
- **Real people:** actions and whereabouts consistent with the record or marked altered.
- **Reader's English:** period words the reader can't infer get context or a plainer word (style guide B14).
