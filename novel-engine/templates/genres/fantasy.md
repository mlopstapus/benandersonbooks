# Genre module: fantasy

## Reader promises
- **Wonder:** at least one thing the reader has never quite seen before, shown early (by chapter 2).
- **A system with rules and a price.** The reader can predict what the magic can and can't do, and feels the cost.
- **A world deeper than the page.** History, cultures and lore exist beyond what is shown, and leak through in details, idioms and assumptions.
- **Earned victories.** The climax is won with rules and tools the reader already understands, at a real cost.
- Sub-genre promises the author names (cozy, grimdark, gaslamp, epic, urban) go here in the book's style guide.

## Core system
**The magic.** Page: `story/system/<magic-name>.md` (from templates/pages/system.md). Add these to the spec table:
- Where does magic come from (a substance, a bloodline, a bargain, a god)? Who controls the source?
- Is it hard (rules the reader learns) or soft (wonder and threat)? If soft, what are the few hard limits that still hold?
- What does using it feel like in the body, during and after?
- Can it heal? Kill? Affect minds? Resurrect? Each needs a yes or no now.
- Does it leave a trace (on the user, the target, the place)? How long does the trace last?
- How common is it? Is it legal? How do ordinary people regard users? What do they believe about it that isn't true?
- What happens at the extremes: a user at full strength, a user out of fuel, two users against each other?
- How does the antagonist's version differ, and what can they do that the hero can't (and vice versa)?

Lesson from *Ash of Verdance*: most mid-book rewrites came from these questions being answered one chapter at a time (healing, how long effects last, range, what another user senses, storage and carriers). Don't start chapter 1 with any of them open if the outline will need them.

## Extra lock questions
- Every set piece and the climax pass the edge-case drill on the system page.
- Fuel and supply: how much the protagonist has at the start, where more comes from, and how much is left at each act break.
- The words on the page: verbs for using the magic, the official name, the slang name, the slur. Ban the generic verbs (pull, push, channel) if the book has its own.
- What the protagonist's community believes about magic (folk sayings, rhymes, taboos) vs. what is true.
- History: the one or two past events (a war, a ban, a discovery) that explain how the world treats magic now.

## Story folders
- `story/system/` (the magic page)
- `story/world/` (overview, places, idioms-and-terms, sets)
- `story/mythology/` (what people believe, kept separate from how magic actually works)
- `story/events/` (history and timeline)

## Research targets
- The model authors' published talks, essays and interviews on worldbuilding and magic systems.
- One or two well-regarded craft essays on magic systems (for example Brandon Sanderson's essays on his laws of magic), summarized with links.
- For historical-flavored settings (gaslamp, medieval), a short note on the real period it echoes: technology, daily life, words to keep and words to avoid.

## Chapter checks
- **Magic violations:** effects beyond the rules, effects bystanders could not observe, wrong verbs, effects lasting longer than allowed, fuel counts that don't add up.
- **New magic:** any capability not on the system page, especially in a climax or escape. Flag it as a proposal; never accept it silently.
- **Lay-reader clarity:** would a first-time reader understand what just happened magically? (style guide B15)
- **Belief vs. truth:** characters state folk beliefs as beliefs, not as narrator fact.
