# Source note — "Getting AI to Write Good Fiction: What Actually Works"
URL: https://slop.sus.cat/fiction/ai-fiction-writing/webapp/index.html
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 via web fetch (summary extraction, not verbatim). Community compilation (as of ~March 2026); figures it cites are unverified.

What works:
- Heavy pre-writing (60–80% of effort on planning; multi-turn discussion of psychology, scene dynamics, sensory detail before prose).
- Banned-word lists (delve, tapestry, testament, vibrant, pivotal, beacon, crucible, realm, embark, resonate, bustling, profound, stark…).
- Positive instructions over negative ("use precise sensory details, vary sentence length") — negative framing can prime the pattern.
- Small chunks: 250–1,200 words per request; edit between chunks; feed edited text back as context.
- Story bible / codex injected into every call.
- Multiple drafts + "kitbashing": generate 3–4 versions, assemble the best lines by hand.
- Cap AI revision at ~3 passes (each pass "blandifies"); use AI to diagnose, rewrite weak passages yourself.
- Budget 1–2 hours of human editing per 1,000 words.
Tells to remove: AI "thesaurus" nouns (tapestry, testament, symphony, labyrinth, beacon…); magic adverbs (quietly, deeply…); body-language clichés (breath hitched, jaw clenched, shiver down the spine, silence stretched, wave of X washed over, couldn't help but…); negative parallelism ("not X — it's Y"); rule-of-three overuse; em-dash overuse; "serves as/stands as"; self-posed rhetorical questions; tailing participles; uniform sentence/paragraph lengths; throat-clearing openers; closing tautologies; synonym cycling; punchy one-line paragraphs; mode-collapse names (Elara, Kai, Luna, Zephyr…), stock places (bustling marketplace, dimly lit tavern).
Limits claimed: weak at subtext, restraint, distinctive voice, comedy, literary fiction; fantasy rated "moderate." Expect ~60–70% of first-draft material usable with disciplined workflow; human judgment is the bottleneck.
Example directives cited: "Avoid sentimentality and unearned epiphany. Do not resolve tension prematurely. Let ambiguity stand." / "Prefer concrete technical specificity over emotional generality."
