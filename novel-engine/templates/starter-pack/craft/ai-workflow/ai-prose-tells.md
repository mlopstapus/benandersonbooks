# AI Prose Tells — Checking-Pass Checklist

Use as a **post-draft scan**, not as a negative prompt (negative lists can prime the very patterns) ([[slop-guide-ai-fiction]]).

## Words
- AI-thesaurus nouns: tapestry, testament, symphony, labyrinth, beacon, crucible, enigma, nexus, mosaic, realm, landscape.
- Verbs/adjectives: delve, embark, underscore, harness, navigate, foster; vibrant, pivotal, intricate, profound, stark, bustling, unwavering.
- Magic adverbs: quietly, deeply, fundamentally, remarkably, softly (in tags).
- Empty modifiers — "very," "a little bit" (Sanderson, BYU 2020 lectures).

## Body-language and emotion clichés
breath hitched / didn't know she was holding · jaw clenched · eyes darkened · heart hammered · shiver down the spine · air crackled with tension · silence stretched · a wave of X washed over · weight settled on shoulders · couldn't help but · voice barely above a whisper · steel in her voice.

## Structures
- "It's not X — it's Y" / "not just X, but Y."
- Tricolons everywhere (rule-of-three abuse).
- Em-dash pileups (keep a few per page).
- "Serves as / stands as" instead of "is."
- Self-answered rhetorical questions ("The result? Devastating.").
- Sentences ending in "…, highlighting/underscoring the…".
- Uniform sentence and paragraph lengths; strings of one-line "punchy" paragraphs.
- Throat-clearing openers ("In the dim light of…").
- Closing epiphanies or summaries that restate what the scene showed (unearned emotion — violates show-don't-tell and the pyramid of abstraction).
- **Tell-then-show** thesis sentences.
- Synonym cycling ("the building… the structure… the edifice").
- **Pronoun drift:** long runs of "he"/"it" with two possible owners (two men in a scene; a sensed "life" vs. a place vs. a feeling). Models over-pronoun to avoid repeating names. Fix with names and plain nouns (see [[style-guide]] B13).
- Dialogue tagged with adverbs instead of carrying emotion itself (Sanderson, BYU 2020 lectures).

## Content
- Default names (Elara, Kai, Luna, Zephyr, Voss…) and stock places (bustling marketplace, dimly lit tavern) ([[max-read-elara-voss]]).
- Over-explained subtext; characters announcing feelings.
- Tension resolved too quickly; conflict softened; everyone reasonable and nice.
- Generic sensory detail ("the smell of rain") instead of specific, unique detail.
