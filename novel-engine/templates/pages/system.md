# {{Core system}}

The thing in this book that must work by rules the reader can trust. For fantasy it is the magic; for sci-fi the technology; for mystery the crime; for historical fiction the period's hard constraints. The genre module (templates/genres/) supplies the specific questions; this page holds the answers. What people *believe* about it lives in `story/mythology/` or the society page, linked from here.

## Name and look
What it is called (official, everyday, slang), checked against famous series in the genre so it doesn't echo them ([[names]]). What it looks, sounds and feels like.

## The hook (one sentence)
Why a reader will think this is cool, terrible or fascinating.

## Premise
A short paragraph: where it comes from and what it does, in plain words.

## Rules (what the reader will understand by the climax)
Number them, and keep the numbers stable (other pages cite "rule 12"). For each: the rule, its state (`canon, date`), and the chapter where the reader first sees it on the page. When a rule changes, rewrite it in place and note what it replaced; move the old version to **Dropped**.

## Limits
What it cannot do, ever.

## What society is taught vs. what is true
The official teaching, the folk beliefs and superstitions, and where each is wrong. A lie about the system that someone powerful keeps alive is a plot engine: who keeps it, why, and which event in the story cracks it.

## Edge-case drill
For every set piece and the climax in the outline: what does the scene need the system to do, and is that legal under the rules above? Record each answer. Run any new proposal (a heist method, a security measure, an escape) through this table before offering it to the author.

## The endgame
Can the system end, be cured or be broken? What would ending it cost, and whom? (In a series this is often the last book's choice.)

## Ties to the world
Economy, law, religion, class, history, institutions.

## Dropped
Rules or mechanics the author replaced, one line each with what replaced them (a sealed vessel dropped for a cup that spills; a location requirement dropped). Keeps them from being re-proposed.
