# Outline: {{TITLE}}

Written ending-first. `book-plan` builds it; `write-chapter` reads one row at a time; canon sync updates rows when an approved chapter changes the plan. For a series, the plan across books lives in [[series-arc]] and this page carries this book's share of each series thread.

**Status:** (who approved what, and when; e.g. "chapter plan approved by the author YYYY-MM-DD; small scene details marked *proposal* can still change at the brief stage".)

# Shape
- **Structure:** prelude? chapter count, named parts, interludes between parts (whose POV, what they show).
- **Length:** the target, or "as long as the story needs" (then the estimate from the chapter plan, in words and pages).
- **POV split:** chapters per POV character, including flashback chapters, and why the split suits this book's focus.
- **Flashbacks** (if any): whose past, in chronological order, each placed just before the present-day beat it sets up.
- **The spine:** the protagonist's defining choice in the past (the wound) and the choice at the climax that echoes it, made knowingly this time. One or two sentences. The reader can see the mirror; nobody in the story needs to name it.

# Promises
What the first chapters promise the reader (tone, the kind of story, the central question). Each must be paid off. For a series, say which promises this book pays and which it hands on.

# Premise
Two sentences: who wants what, what stands in the way, what it will cost.

# Plot threads (end state first)
For each thread: where it ends in this book, then the steps that get it there, by chapter number. For a series, link the series thread it belongs to. A thread the reader should be able to *watch* progress (a thing being built, a count running down, a skill learned) gets a **progress ladder**: its state in each chapter that touches it.

## A. <main thread>: ends with …
- Ladder (if any): nothing (ch 3) → first success (ch 7) → … → final state (ch N).

# Antagonist plan (off the page)
What the antagonist does regardless of the hero, what they know when, why they escalate, and (for a layered antagonist) which layer of their motive this book reveals and which it keeps back.

# Clue and payoff plan
Every clue, setup, plant and hidden-plan preparation the ending (or a later book) depends on. Mark hints that are only suggestions as *(proposed)*.
| Setup / clue | Planted in | Points to | Paid off in | Reader should suspect by |
|---|---|---|---|---|

# Set pieces and action
The big scenes, each turning on a different rule of the core system or a different kind of tension. Check the mix: are there enough moments of physical danger for the genre and the author's taste, and do repeated kinds (two heists, two chases) play differently?
| Ch | Scene | Kind (heist, fight, chase, stealth, standoff, ordeal, talk-down, reveal) | Rule or setup it turns on |
|---|---|---|---|

# Climax
Beat by beat, using only locked rules and things the reader has already seen. Note any cut-away (the plan hidden from the reader) and where the reveal lands, including from another POV.

# Ending and denouement
End state of every thread and every main character's arc. Epilogue, if any. For a series: what this book leaves open, and the hook into the next.

# Chapter list
One row per chapter. Put part headings as their own rows. Every chapter changes something.
| Ch | Title (working) | POV | Day | Purpose (what changes) | Threads | Words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

# Chapter beats
One short section per chapter (or per prelude and interlude), built part by part with the author:
- 3–6 bullets: what happens, in order, and why.
- **Plant:** any clue or preparation placed here (also in the clue table).
- **Hook:** how the chapter ends (a turn, a question, a cut-away).
- Scene details the author hasn't approved stay marked *proposal*; the brief settles them.

# Open (for the author)
Questions the outline leaves open, each with the chapter that needs the answer.
