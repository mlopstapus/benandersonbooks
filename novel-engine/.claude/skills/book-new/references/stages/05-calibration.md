# Stage 5: voice calibration

Adjectives ("rich", "earnest", "tight") mean different things to different people. An A/B test settles them in ten minutes, and it settles them better than another round of questions.

## Default: two versions of the real opening
1. **Pick the scene:** the protagonist doing what they do in the book's opening (a routine job, a typical day), about one page. If the plot isn't planned yet, use a scene that shows the protagonist's skill and the core system at small scale. Invented details (the object, the setting, side characters) are placeholders and say so.
2. **Write two versions (A and B), each leaning toward a different model author's strengths** (for example, A with the protagonist's attitude in the narration and the punchline theirs; B quieter, competence shown step by step, drier humor). Keep the plot of the scene identical. Follow the style guide in both. If there is only one model author, lean the versions on the two dimensions the taste interview left most open.
3. **Ask:** which one, and what the author likes about it. Answers like "I like the character in A" are enough; ask a follow-up only if the answer splits ("A's character, B's description").
4. **Settle the scope of the voice.** Ask whether the winning style is the book's one voice, with each POV character coloring it through what they notice and how they judge it (how Sanderson and Flanagan handle many POVs), or whether some chapters should sound deliberately different. Default to one voice.

## Optional: dimension pairs
If the A/B leaves something unclear, write short pairs (120–180 words each) of one neutral scene, changing **one** dimension per pair and keeping everything else the same:
1. **Humor:** earnest and sincere vs. dry and wry.
2. **Description:** lean and quick vs. rich and multi-sensory.
3. **Interiority:** action and dialogue with almost no thought vs. frequent introspection.
Also possible: dialogue-heavy vs. narration-heavy; close vs. distant POV. Use invented names from outside the book. Answers like "B but less" are useful; ask what "less" means with an example line.

## Record
- Create `craft/calibration.md` from `templates/pages/calibration.md`: the result in the author's words, the winning sample verbatim, **what makes it work** (four or five drafting rules drawn from the sample), any dimension rules as a table, and **still to check**: a short test page for each other POV character, in the same voice, before their first chapter.
- Style guide **section H:** the result in two lines and a link to the calibration page. Point the drafting preamble (section D) and the self-check (section E) at the sample.
- Under "Calibration results" in `reader-taste.md`, one line.
- Note on the protagonist's character page how their attitude shows in the narration (from the sample).

Commit `style: calibration`, log it. The book is ready for `/book-plan`.
