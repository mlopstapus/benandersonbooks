# Stage 5: voice calibration

Adjectives ("rich", "earnest", "tight") mean different things to different people. An A/B test settles them in ten minutes, and it settles them better than another round of questions.

## Make the passages
Write three pairs of short passages (120–180 words each). Use the same small scene for every pair, a scene that fits the book's genre and POV but isn't from the book's plot (a character arriving somewhere and noticing something wrong is a good default). In each pair change **one** dimension and keep everything else the same:

1. **Humor:** A earnest and sincere, B dry and wry.
2. **Description:** A lean and quick, B rich and multi-sensory (smell, sound, touch, temperature, specific concrete details).
3. **Interiority:** A action and dialogue with almost no thought, B frequent introspection in italics.

Follow the style guide while writing them. Label them 1A/1B, 2A/2B, 3A/3B. Use different invented names from anything in the book.

Optional fourth pair if the taste interview left it unclear: dialogue-heavy vs. narration-heavy, or close vs. distant POV.

## Ask
Show the pairs and ask for each: A, B, or somewhere between (and what they'd change). Answers like "B but less" are useful; ask what "less" means with an example line.

## Record
Add a table to style guide **section H** and to `reader-taste.md` under "Calibration results":

| Dimension | Pick | Rule for drafting |
|---|---|---|

Each rule is concrete enough to check ("one to three lines of italic thought per beat, ending in a decision; never a paragraph of rumination").

Commit `style: calibration`, log it. The book is ready for `/book-plan`.
