# Mode: trim

Typical goal: about 10% shorter, chapter at a time. The usual cuts are recaps, doubled beats, explanations the reader doesn't need, and stage business.

1. Agree the scope with the author: the target percentage, what must not be cut (setting words, idioms, the author's lines, climax chapters), and whether small trims can be applied without review. (The author may start by reviewing every trim and later authorize small ones; record that in `manuscript/decisions.md`.)
2. Per chapter, read in full and mark candidates, cheapest first:
   - recaps and re-explanations (a rule stated twice, a retold scene);
   - repeated beats and descriptions (see the repeats mode);
   - sentences that tell what the scene already shows;
   - stage business that doesn't characterize (doors, coats, walking from room to room);
   - whole minor scenes that don't change anything (propose these separately as scene trims).
3. Tight chapters (climaxes, the author's most-shaped chapters) may need no trims. Say so and move on; don't cut for the sake of the number.
4. Report words before → after per chapter.

Scene-level trims always go to the author first. After a trim pass, run the fresh-eyes continuity check on the trimmed chapters (a cut can remove a setup a later chapter relies on).
