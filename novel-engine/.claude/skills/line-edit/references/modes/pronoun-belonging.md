# Mode: pronoun and belonging

Two passes, read in full, chapter by chapter. In *Ash of Verdance* a single pronoun sweep over 15 chapters made about 120 edits, and nearly all of them came from a beta reader stopping to ask "who is 'he' here?"

## Pronoun pass (style guide B9)
Every he/him/his/she/her/it/they must have one obvious owner on a first read.
- **Two people of the same gender in a paragraph → names.** A pronoun may follow a name only if no other same-gender person was named since. Re-name after the other person speaks, and at the start of a paragraph where the subject changes.
- **"It" points at the nearest noun.** Check what that noun is. If "it" lands on the wrong thing, or stands for a vague situation ("It would always be something"), name the thing.
- **Non-human referents:** sensed presences, creatures, magical effects. Once the reader knows who or what it is, use the name, unless the point is that the POV can't tell.
- **Scene openings:** the first sentence after a scene break names its subject. Never open on He, She or It.
- Prefer a plain noun ("the lock", "the cloth") over a clever substitute. No synonym cycling.
- After the read-through, run a heuristic scan as a backstop: paragraphs starting with He/She/His/Her right after a paragraph that last named another same-gender person; scenes opening on a pronoun. Check each hit by hand.

## Belonging pass (style guide B10): "which sentence doesn't belong here?"
Read each paragraph like a reading-test question. Look for:
- an aside dropped between a cause and its effect;
- a reaction placed before the thing it reacts to, or against the wrong line;
- a sentence that contradicts its neighbor;
- a character described again when the reader already has the description;
- a detail that disagrees with the same moment in another chapter;
- doubled or garbled wording;
- words the reader is likely to misread (B14) and any words the author has flagged (style guide section I and B14's list).

Pronoun fixes are applied directly. Belonging findings are taste calls unless they are plainly errors (a contradiction, garbled wording).
