# Mode: dialogue

LLM drafts tend to make everyone talk the same way, usually clipped and efficient. This pass gives each speaker their own voice back.

For each scene with dialogue:
- **Who is terse?** Only characters whose personality is terseness speak in two- or three-word lines (the distinct-voices rule). Everyone else gets full sentences with their own texture: a detail, a reason, a bit of history or humor. Keep a short line only when the shortness is the point (someone going quiet, a fight beat).
- **Voice check against character pages:** diction, where their images come from, how they argue, catchphrases or habits (a rhyme, an oath). Would you know who is speaking without the tag?
- **Exposition:** break any speech that explains into real back-and-forth with interruptions and reactions. Cut "as you know".
- **Tags:** "said" for most; beats instead of tags where they ground the scene; no adverbs doing the emotion's job.
- **Logic:** does each line answer what was actually said? Is the order of lines natural (a reaction after the line it reacts to)?

Rewrites of dialogue are taste calls; propose them in batches per chapter. When the author supplies lines, use them verbatim.
