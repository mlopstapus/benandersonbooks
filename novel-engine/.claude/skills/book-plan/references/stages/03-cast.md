# Stage 3: cast

One character per round. Leads first (the protagonist, then any co-leads), then the antagonist and the one or two closest supporting characters, then supporting, then a list of minor roles. Check `story/world/names.md` before proposing any name.

## Leads
Use `templates/pages/character.md`. A lead's page carries the most, because their arcs run the book (or the series). Work through:
- **Role and why the story needs them:** what only they can do or get (an access, a skill, an outsider's view, a blind spot of the antagonist's they can exploit).
- **Model and differences.** If the author names a favorite character as a model (stage 2 of book-new recorded these), say what to keep and propose three or four specific differences so the lead is their own person (draws people in instead of pushing them away; avoids fights instead of picking them; embellishes instead of lying). Name the famous characters in the genre they must not slide into, and how they differ.
- **Why we like them, quirks, weaknesses and struggles.** Two or three of each, concrete. Give at least one lead a physical limit that costs them in action, and at least one a struggle to overcome (anxiety, pride, fear).
- **Relationship to the truth.** Make each lead handle truth differently (bends it, can't lie, withholds). Write the contrast on each page; it powers scenes between them.
- **The wound, as a choice** (see stage 2), and the lie it taught them → the truth they learn.
- **Past, with locked ages** (age at the wound, years since). For a focus lead with flashback chapters, plan the past in enough detail to write those chapters in order.
- **Beats per book** (series) or per act, matching the series arc and the spine.
- **Avoid trope pairings** the author flags (the easygoing boy and the by-the-book girl), and record them in Craft notes.
- **Romance** (if any): the stage in each book, within `content_lines`.

## Main and supporting characters
Snapshot; want, need, wound; why they can't have it yet; their personal connection to the plot. Then:
- **Arc from start to end**, matching the ending in the outline.
- Backstory: locked facts only. **Name the off-page people who matter** (a dead spouse, a parent, a mentor, the people the protagonist lost). In *Ash of Verdance* one such character had no name until chapter 21, when the book's second-biggest scene needed one.
- Voice: diction, where their images come from, how they argue, two or three sample lines. Decide who is terse and who isn't (the distinct-voices rule), and how strongly each POV character colors the narration ([[calibration]]).
- How they use or are affected by the core system.
- **Knows / doesn't know** at the start: every secret in the book (and the series), from this character's side.
- Relationships to the rest of the cast.

Supporting characters can be shorter, but always get motive, voice and knows/doesn't know.

## Weight
Decide each named character's **weight**: main (real page time, a page of their own), or walk-on (a name and a one-line sketch in a table on the relevant hub, such as the heads of the other guilds). Walk-on sketches stay proposed until the author approves them; the names can be canon on their own.

## Minor roles
A table in `story/world/idioms-and-terms.md` (name, role, where they appear). Name them deliberately; never accept model-default names (see `craft/ai-workflow/ai-prose-tells.md`), and never reuse names from `off_limits`.

## Close
Add every name to `story/world/names.md` and the cast map in the book's `CLAUDE.md`. Check the cast against `craft/scope.md`. Commit `plan: cast`.
