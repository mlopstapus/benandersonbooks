# Stage 0: series arc (series only)

Skip for a standalone. For a series, a rough version comes first, because almost every book-1 decision either plants something for a later book or closes a door on it. It doesn't have to be finished before the other stages; it has to exist, and it gets sharper every session.

Write to `manuscript/series-arc.md` (from `templates/pages/series-arc.md`).

## 1. Shape: one question per book
Ask for (or offer) a question each book answers, in a few words ("Can anything grow?", "Who is hiding what?", "What will we give up?"). Under each: what the book wins, and what that win costs or leaves open. A win that can't last is a good book-1 ending: it proves something is possible and sets the next book's goal.

If focus rotates (`series.focus_rotation`), assign each book's **focus lead**. The focus lead gets flashback chapters into their past that book; every lead keeps a point of view in every book. This means each lead's past must be planned early enough to write their flashbacks.

## 2. Series threads
List the three to six threads that run the whole series. Each is **hinted** in book 1, **turns** in a middle book and **pays off** in the last. Typical threads:
- **The escalation ladder:** what the heroes achieve each book (breakthroughs, alliances, discoveries), each step cheaper or stronger than the last, each threatening someone new. Fill the ladder table.
- **The antagonist's layers** (next section).
- **The buried secret:** the thing the whole series is about uncovering. How the heroes find it (often by its absence: a gap in a record, a scar, a missing person), and the book where each piece lands.
- **The force against them:** how the opposition's muscle escalates book by book, and whether it turns.
- **The leads' personal threads,** romance included.

For each thread, write the book-by-book steps in one line each. Don't plan book 3's chapters; plan its destination.

## 3. The antagonist, layer by layer
A series antagonist, especially an institution, holds up best when its motive is revealed one layer per book:
1. **A sincere layer** the reader half agrees with (they would lose something they truly love). Give it a face: one character who believes it and whom the book honors.
2. **A self-interest layer** (power, money, survival of the institution).
3. **A buried crime or secret** that the first two layers were protecting.

Ask which layer each book reveals, and what the heroes do that makes it come out. Then list **their lies** (things they tell everyone that aren't true, including lies about how the core system works) and the book where each cracks. Then **their muscle:** who enforces for them, honestly or not, and whether and when that force turns, with the trigger.

## 4. The endgame and its cost
What is the last book's final choice? The best ones cost every lead something personal, and answer the series' opening image (a prologue's warning, a first-chapter promise). If the core system can end, decide now whether the endgame ends it and what that takes from whom; it shapes every lead's arc.

## 5. The leads and how they meet
- How many leads the series needs (three is a common, sturdy number). One line each, linking their pages (built in stage 3).
- **Their relationship to the truth:** make each one different (one bends it, one can't lie, one withholds). It drives scenes for three books.
- **How they meet in book 1:** each wants something only another can give. Write the trade.
- **Key moments table:** for each lead, two to four moments per book.
- **Romance** (if any): its stage per book and the moment it resolves, inside `content_lines`.
- **Rifts and confessions:** secrets between leads, the book each comes out, and whether it is confessed or discovered (confession usually completes an arc; discovery usually starts one).

## 6. Handoffs and the parking lot
- What each book leaves for the next: the open question, the object or place that is the way back in, the goal the next book opens with.
- **For later planning sessions:** questions that can wait, each tagged with the book that needs it. Don't push for answers the author says can wait.

## Close
Play back the shape in five lines (one per book, plus the endgame). Approve what's canon, mark the rest leaning or proposed, commit `plan: series arc`. Come back to this page whenever a book-1 decision touches a later book.
