# Stage 4: world

Only as much world as the story will touch, plus enough depth underneath that details feel consistent. Lore can stay off the page; it still has to exist.

## Ask, in rounds
1. **Overview** (`story/world/overview.md`): the setting in a paragraph; era feel and technology level; the one physical hook that makes this place distinct; the one or two cultural focuses that matter to this story.
2. **History** (`story/events/`): the one or two past events that explain how the world treats the core system and the main conflict (a war, a ban, a plague, a discovery). Who knows the truth vs. the official story.
3. **Places** (`templates/pages/place.md`): the city or region and its districts, then each place a chapter will use. Sensory signature for each. Travel times between key places.
4. **Recurring sets** (`story/world/sets.md`): the three to six locations the book returns to (the protagonist's home, the workplace, the antagonist's house, a meeting place). Lock the layout: rooms, doors, which entrance people use, what is where. *Ash of Verdance* needed fixes for a front door vs. back door and a kitchen range vs. a parlor fireplace.
5. **Beliefs** (`story/mythology/` if the module creates it): what people believe about the core system and history, kept separate from what is true.
6. **Language** (`story/world/idioms-and-terms.md`): place names, oaths, slang, units, forms of address. Check each against the reader's English (`dialect`, `period_words`).
7. **Map** (optional): a simple SVG map in `story/world/` for the places the book uses. Render it to check labels don't overlap.

Commit `plan: world`.
