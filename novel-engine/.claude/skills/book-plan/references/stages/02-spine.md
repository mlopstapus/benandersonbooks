# Stage 2: spine

The spine is the story in a page: what it promises, who wants what, who stands in the way, and how it ends. Write the ending before the middle. For a series, this is book N's spine inside the series arc: check each answer against `manuscript/series-arc.md`.

## Ask, in rounds
1. **Premise:** who wants what, what stands in the way, what it will cost. Draft two sentences together.
2. **Protagonist:** who they are when the story opens, their competence, their flaw, and the secret or pressure they carry. (Full page comes in stage 3; here just enough to drive the spine.)
3. **The wound and the mirror.** What choice in the protagonist's past defines them? A wound they *chose* (and hid) carries more than one done to them: "I was caught" is bad luck, "I chose" is guilt. Then find the climax choice that echoes it: the same kind of choice, made knowingly this time. Write the mirror in one or two sentences in the outline's **Shape**. Nobody in the story needs to name it.
4. **Antagonist:** who, what they want, and **why they believe they're right**. What they do off the page. Iterate until the motive is personal and specific. (In *Ash of Verdance* the villain was rebuilt three times before his motive held.) For a series, which layer of the antagonist's motive this book reveals (series arc), and what it keeps back.
5. **Ending:** where the protagonist ends up, what it cost, what the antagonist's fate is, and how each main relationship ends. Is there a denouement or epilogue? Decide now; adding one late reshuffles the last chapters. For a series: what this book wins, what it leaves open, and the hook into the next book.
6. **Plant, don't explain.** In a series, book 1 can show *what* happened without explaining *why*. Decide which answers the protagonist (and the reader) only wonder about by the end, and which book explains them. A question the reader feels harder than the protagonist does is a good book-1 ending.
7. **Stakes by act:** what the protagonist stands to lose at each act break (legal, social, personal). Set the penalties the plot depends on (what getting caught costs), because they decide how heavy each choice is.
8. **Threads:** the main thread and the subplots the scope allows. For each, its end state first, then the main steps. For a series, which series thread each one carries.

## Write to `manuscript/outline.md`
Fill **Shape** (the spine sentence; structure comes in stage 6), **Promises**, **Premise**, **Plot threads (end state first)**, **Antagonist plan (off the page)** (a first version; stage 5 makes it day-by-day), and **Ending and denouement**. Mark proposals. Keep the author's lines verbatim.

## Check against scope
Compare thread count and implied length with `craft/scope.md`. If the spine is too big for the form, say so now and offer what to cut or merge. If the author has set no target length, estimate one from the threads and say it in words and pages.

Commit `plan: spine`.
