# Stage 2: spine

The spine is the story in a page: what it promises, who wants what, who stands in the way, and how it ends. Write the ending before the middle.

## Ask, in rounds
1. **Premise:** who wants what, what stands in the way, what it will cost. Draft two sentences together.
2. **Protagonist:** who they are when the story opens, their competence, their flaw, and the secret or pressure they carry. (Full page comes in stage 3; here just enough to drive the spine.)
3. **Antagonist:** who, what they want, and **why they believe they're right**. What they do off the page. Iterate until the motive is personal and specific. (In *Ash of Verdance* the villain was rebuilt three times before his motive held.)
4. **Ending:** where the protagonist ends up, what it cost, what the antagonist's fate is, and how each main relationship ends. Is there a denouement or epilogue? Decide now; adding one late reshuffles the last chapters.
5. **Stakes by act:** what the protagonist stands to lose at each act break (legal, social, personal).
6. **Threads:** the main thread and the subplots the scope allows. For each, its end state first, then the main steps.

## Write to `manuscript/outline.md`
Fill **Promises**, **Premise**, **Plot threads (end state first)**, **Antagonist plan (off the page)** (a first version; stage 5 makes it day-by-day), and **Ending and denouement**. Mark proposals. Keep the author's lines verbatim.

## Check against scope
Compare thread count and implied length with `craft/scope.md`. If the spine is too big for the form, say so now and offer what to cut or merge.

Commit `plan: spine`.
