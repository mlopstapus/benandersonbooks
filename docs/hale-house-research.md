# 6 Wicker Street: manuscript research and tour design

Collected 29 October 2026 from the local manuscript and story wiki. **This working document includes plot spoilers.** It is outside `site/` and therefore outside the Vercel output. The public tour uses physical descriptions, not the events attached to them.

## Source priority and the chosen moment

The prose in `novel-engine/books/ash-of-verdance/manuscript/chapters/` is the primary source. The world notes supply the explicit house type and travel estimates. These notes include an old proposal header and some obsolete chapter references, so specific prose takes priority where the two differ. Chapter numbers below refer to the current filenames, not older continuity summaries.

The model represents the early-book house: a cold unused front parlor, a working kitchen, a sticking front door, and the old pencil marks. It does **not** use the ending's inquiry office, newly lit parlor fire, new row of tally marks, or repaired door. Physical facts first described late in the book (lace, for example) can describe the earlier house without importing the later events.

## Changing details: avoid mixing chapters

- **Early book:** parlor unused, no fire, door sticks, stash intact/changes as sap used, old tally marks only.
- **Ch12–13:** warrant/search, tins removed, sentimental cloth returned, bedroom searched and tidied by Gideon, kitchen fire unlit.
- **Ch14–15:** range built very hot, both scuttles used, blankets on floor, quilt dropped on third stair, plate left for Callum. Do not use these as timeless decorative staging.
- **Ch18–19:** Ida's twelve black notebooks on kitchen table in Hobb's brown paper with string undone; removed after her arrest. These are plot evidence, not normal household decor.
- **Ch25:** range warm, kettle on rail, water by hearth, Callum adds one new mark below the old 374.
- **Ch26:** parlor becomes inquiry office, table and two chairs move in, ink/pencil/ruled paper added, card **G. HALE. INQUIRIES.** in front window, loose board nailed down (Thursday Dec18), front door planed (Saturday Dec20), ten new tally marks. This is a separate scene variant, not part of present prototype.

## Interpretation ledger

- Illustration footprint approximately 4.6 × 7 world units and two storeys. Units are drawing proportions, not canonical meters.
- Front parlor faces street; kitchen faces yard; hall/stair to one side. Adjacency supported; orientation and dimensions inferred.
- Sash windows, narrow brick terrace, slate roof, chimneys, drainpipe, gas lamp, stone curb, neighbouring houses, slate/brick hatch marks: period/setting interpretation, not exact manuscript facts.
- Range along left wall; table central; cupboard near rear window; shelf and clock on inner partition; peg on side wall by back door: furnishing layout inferred.
- Yard walls, paving, simple plank gate, privy construction, sparse weeds, and small storage box: inferred.
- Wicker Street is not labeled on `site/map.jpg`; marker at 64% horizontal / 69% vertical indicates **approximate Southbank placement**, not a canon coordinate. Visible disclaimer in map dialog and accessible label on homepage marker.
- View compass is labelled **VIEW**, not north: model orientation to the map has not been established.
- This is real 3D with orbit and fixed eye-level viewpoints. Free walking, door physics, and sound remain outside this prototype; the parlor and both upstairs bedrooms are now enterable.

### Room-rendering correction

The initial six-location revision constructed all interiors but only added the kitchen and exterior to the Three.js world. The parlor and bedrooms therefore selected correctly in the notes while rendering an empty world. `mountHouse` now mounts the exterior and every interior; the persistent geometry check verifies each root’s parent is the rendered scene. A DOM runtime check also imports the actual app, runs its first frame, selects all six locations, and checks each selected root reaches the renderer with visible meshes. That runtime check stubs WebGL and therefore does not prove GPU rendering.

Texture loading now times out after eight seconds and falls back to procedural pen textures. The loading indicator remains visible until a frame renders. Custom-shader failure triggers a retry using standard textured materials; render exceptions show the descriptive fallback. Entry and dependent app modules use `room-fix-3` query versions so a browser cannot combine the new controller with cached old model exports.

### Graphics compatibility

A failed graphics startup previously displayed the city map, which obscured the real failure. The tour now tries an actual Three.js SVG renderer when WebGL cannot start or the GPU renderer fails. It projects the same house and room geometry into interactive vector paths with pen patterns; the camera, room graph, sidebar and door picking are retained. Software mode uses simpler geometry and immediate camera changes to keep it lighter. `/tour/?renderer=svg` forces this path for testing. It does not claim to reproduce WebGL texture mapping, shadows, or shader effects.

`SVGRenderer.js` and `Projector.js` are vendored from the same Three.js 0.186.1 package under its MIT license. SVGRenderer has one documented `faceStyle` extension so the application can supply ink patterns without changing projection. Normal WebGL rendering still uses the generated texture atlas; the software drawing uses self-contained vector patterns and requires no GPU shader. Errors remaining after recovery appear under Technical details, and the old map placeholder is removed.

### Connected-house revision, 29 October 2026

The prototype now uses one shared coordinate system: parlor and kitchen on the ground floor, Gideon at the front upstairs and Callum at the back. An open passage runs beside the rooms, with a full sixteen-tread staircase and an upper landing. The room sizes, stair direction, number of treads, and doorway placements remain artistic interpretation. The parlor doorway stands open for exploration; this is not a claim about its door's position in any particular scene.

Navigation follows a graph of standing positions through those openings. Picking another room follows the hall and stairs rather than replacing the visible room model. A ground-floor/upstairs cutaway is the default, with an explicit switch to eye level. Exterior doors are open; the kitchen door retains its pencil marks. Large floating entrance labels remain absent; floor rings reveal their names on hover/focus, with equivalent text controls available in the guide.

Tooltip titles and manuscript descriptions use explicit dark text and support focus as well as hover.
